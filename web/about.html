Ranking wildfire risk across the Mount Kenya forest

Wildfires are a growing threat to forests, water supplies and livelihoods, yet most fire management in Kenya reacts after a fire has started. This prototype uses free satellite data and machine learning to rank which 1 km squares of the Mount Kenya forest are most at risk in each 8-day period, so rangers and conservancies can decide where to look first.

How it works

Three steps turn satellite readings into a risk map.

Step 1

Satellite data

Every 8 days we gather vegetation greenness and moisture, surface temperature and rainfall for each 1 km square of the forest, plus its elevation and slope.

→
Step 2

Machine learning model

A Random Forest learns from 25 years of history which conditions came before new fires, and is tested on years it has never seen.

→
Step 3

Risk map

Each square is ranked from Low to Very High and shown on the map, together with the conditions behind its rank.

Data sources

All inputs are free and openly available through Google Earth Engine.

MODIS satellite imagery

NASA sensors give vegetation greenness (NDVI), vegetation moisture (NDMI) and land surface temperature.

CHIRPS rainfall

Daily rainfall estimates, added up per period and over recent weeks to show dry spells.

SRTM elevation

A global terrain survey, used for each square's elevation and slope.

MODIS burned-area records

Where and when land burned since 2001. These are the real fires the model learns from.

The study area follows the official protected-area boundary (WDPA) around Mount Kenya.

How well does it work?

Tested on years the model had never seen.

1,996one-kilometre squares
25years of history (2001–2025)
849recorded fire events
75%of new fires fell in the top 5% of ranked squares

The colours are a ranking, not a forecast. Even among Very High squares, a new fire follows in the next 8 days only about 1 time in 120. That is around 30 times the average, which is why the map is meant to show where to look first, not to predict a particular fire.

Limitations

What this prototype can and cannot tell you.

Coarse resolutionEach square is 1 km across, so squares at the forest edge mix forest with other land.
Cloud gapsClouds hide the ground. Missing readings are filled with the last clear one for up to 3 periods.
Summit zone excludedThe high moorland and peaks inside the national park are outside the study area.
People are not modelledMost ignitions are started by people, and the model cannot see them. Satellites can also miss small fires.
Not live yetThe map replays predictions for past periods. It is not yet connected to current satellite data.
